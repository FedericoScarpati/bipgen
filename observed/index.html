    Observed graphs¶

    From a graph to a specification¶

    spec_from_graph builds a kernel-model specification from a biadjacency matrix (BCN as rows, TGT as columns):

    import bipgen

adj = bipgen.read_edge_list("edges.csv", bcn="bcn", tgt="tgt")
spec = bipgen.spec_from_graph(adj, scale=0.15)
spec.save("observed.yaml")      # writes observed.yaml and observed.npz

    What is read off the graph:

    • the number of nodes and the degree sequence of each side, after binarizing the matrix and dropping isolated nodes; they become an empirical degree section;
    • if positions of some nodes are given, the cell occupancies and the correlogram of their density (see below); positions are then drawn with the Metropolis method.

    What is not inferred, and has to be chosen: the kernel scale and the background.

    Density measured on points¶

    With positions, spec_from_graph takes the 2D points, maps their bounding box to \([-1, 1]^2\), counts the points in the cells of a grid with n_bins cells per axis inside the disk, and computes the spatial correlation between nearby cells (Moran's I) of the counts at lags 1 to n_lags. This assumes that the known positions are a random sample of the nodes, and that BCN and TGT share the same density, since both are drawn from it.

    Correction for partial positions

    When the known points are only part of the nodes, counting noise attenuates the correlation. spec_from_graph uses the corrected correlogram, multiplied by \(\mathrm{var}/(\mathrm{var} - \mathrm{mean})\) (Poisson counts around the density), or the raw one when the correction is not usable. The correction tends to overestimate the correlation when the known points are a large fraction of the nodes, and is unstable when cells hold few points.

    Comparing an observed graph with an ensemble¶

    ensemble = [bipgen.generate(spec, seed=s) for s in range(20)]
table = bipgen.compare(adj, ensemble)
bipgen.plot_comparison(table)
bipgen.degree_ks(adj, ensemble)

    statistics computes, on the observed graph and on each realization alike: numbers of nodes and edges, mean, median, 90th percentile and maximum degree of each side, and the fraction of nodes in the largest connected component. Only nodes with at least one edge are counted. compare reports the observed value, the ensemble mean and standard deviation, the z-score and the percentile of the observed value in the ensemble: a Monte Carlo goodness-of-fit check. degree_ks compares the degree distributions with the two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance, next to the distance between two realizations, which is what sampling alone produces.

    compare also accepts a stored summary instead of a graph, such as bipgen.presets.observed(name).
