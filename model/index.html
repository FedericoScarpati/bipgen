    The model¶

    This page describes how bipgen builds one graph, in four steps: node positions, target degrees, geometric edges and background edges. For each step, the table gives the specification section that sets it and the attributes of the generated graph that hold its result; the text names each key where it acts. The last sections cover reproducibility and known limitations.

    Step Set by Result
    Positions dim, sizes, positions graph.pos_bcn, graph.pos_tgt
    Target degrees degrees graph.target_degrees
    Geometric edges edges graph.geometric
    Background edges background graph.background
    Reproducibility seed graph.seed

    The graph also keeps the specification it was built from, in graph.spec, and the diagnostics of each step, such as the correlogram reached or the Sinkhorn iterations, in graph.info, together with the seconds each step took (graph.info["timing"]).

    Positions¶

    sizes.n_bcn BCN and sizes.n_tgt TGT are placed in the domain set by dim: the unit disk (dim: 2) or the unit ball (dim: 3). Lengths are in units of the domain radius. positions.method chooses one of three methods.

    • Uniform (uniform, the default). Points are spread uniformly over the domain.

    • Spatially correlated density (metropolis). Nodes are spread over a grid of cells, with a chosen variability of the counts between cells and a chosen correlation between nearby cells. BCN and TGT are drawn together from this density, then split at random.

      1. Grid: density.n_bins cells per axis, keeping the cells whose centre lies in the domain; 20 by default in 2D, 12 in 3D, where there are many more cells.
      2. Counts: one count per cell, drawn from density.occupancy, rescaled so that the counts add up to the number of nodes. With {source: gamma, cv: ...} the counts follow a Gamma distribution with coefficient of variation cv; with {source: file, key: ...} they are resampled from the array key of the arrays file, for instance measured on another graph (see Observed graphs).
      3. Arrangement: simulated annealing swaps the counts of two cells at a time, to bring the correlogram \(\rho_k\) towards the target \(\rho_k^\ast\) given by density.correlogram.
        • \(\rho_k\) is the spatial correlation between nearby cells (Moran's I [1]), computed over the pairs of cells \(k\) cells apart (distance between centres, rounded): lag 1 is the 8 surrounding cells in 2D, the 18 sharing a face or an edge in 3D.
        • A swap that changes the error \(E = \sum_k (\rho_k - \rho_k^\ast)^2\) by \(\Delta E\) is accepted with probability \(\min(1, e^{-\Delta E / T})\) (Metropolis rule). The temperature \(T\) starts where a worsening swap of average size is accepted with probability 0.8, and decreases geometrically to a thousandth of that over density.sweeps sweeps, of one proposed swap per cell.
        • Swaps keep the set of counts and change only their arrangement. The correlogram reached is stored in graph.info["correlogram"].
      4. Points: drawn uniformly inside each cell; points outside the domain are discarded, then random points are removed down to the exact total. The density is constant within each cell.

    • Given positions (file). Positions are read from two arrays of the arrays file, named by positions.bcn and positions.tgt, of shape (n_bcn, dim) and (n_tgt, dim); from Python, arrays can also be a dictionary of arrays. Positions are used as given, in the same units as edges.scale.

    Target degrees¶

    Target degrees are used only by the kernel model, and are stored in graph.target_degrees. degrees.distribution sets the distribution, and degrees.bcn and degrees.tgt its parameters on each side:

    • lognormal: median median and standard deviation of the logarithm sigma; the TGT side takes only sigma;
    • constant: every BCN gets value;
    • empirical: positive values taken from the array key of the arrays file, resampled with replacement when their number differs from the number of nodes.

    The BCN degrees are rounded and clipped to \([1, n_\mathrm{tgt}]\). The TGT values give only a shape: they are rescaled to the BCN total, rounded, clipped to \([1, n_\mathrm{bcn}]\) and adjusted by one unit at a time until the two totals are equal. With uniform and metropolis positions the order of the nodes carries no spatial information, so degrees are independent of position. With file positions, an empirical array as long as the side keeps its order: the \(i\)-th value goes to the \(i\)-th node.

    Geometric edges¶

    Geometric edges depend on the distance \(d_{ij}\) between a BCN \(i\) and a TGT \(j\), and are stored in graph.geometric. Two models are available, set by edges.model:

    • kernel: each pair is linked at random, with probability \(P_{ij} = \min(1, \theta_i K(d_{ij}) \theta_j)\): the kernel \(K\) decays with distance, and the weights \(\theta\) of the nodes are fitted so that the expected degrees match the targets;
    • knn: each BCN is linked to its k nearest TGT, by distance alone.

    Kernel model¶

    Each pair \((i, j)\) is linked independently with probability

    \[ P_{ij} = \min\left(1,\ \theta_i \, K(d_{ij}) \, \theta_j\right), \]

    where \(K\) is the kernel (see The kernel) and the node weights \(\theta\) solve

    \[ \sum_j \theta_i K(d_{ij}) \theta_j = k_i, \qquad \sum_i \theta_i K(d_{ij}) \theta_j = k_j , \]

    for every BCN \(i\) and every TGT \(j\), so that expected degrees match the targets \(k\) (before the clipping at 1, see Known limitations). The weights are found by Sinkhorn scaling [2], alternating \(\theta_i \leftarrow k_i / \sum_j K_{ij}\theta_j\) and \(\theta_j \leftarrow k_j / \sum_i K_{ij}\theta_i\), with \(K_{ij} = K(d_{ij})\), until no weight changes by more than edges.sinkhorn.tol, for at most edges.sinkhorn.max_iter iterations. graph.info records the iterations (sinkhorn_iterations), whether they converged (sinkhorn_converged) and the largest relative gap between expected and target degrees before clipping (sinkhorn_max_error).

    The kernel¶

    The kernel \(K(d)\) is the profile of a link in distance. Each node has a weight \(\theta\), fitted to its target degree, and the kernel sets how far its links reach. A constant factor in \(K\) is absorbed by the weights, so only its shape matters, and \(K(0) = 1\). edges.kernel names the kernel. The only kernel in this version is the Gaussian:

    • Gaussian (gaussian):

      \[ K(d) = e^{-(d/L)^2}, \]

      where \(L\) is edges.scale, in units of the domain radius. The kernel depends on the distance only through \(d/L\): it falls to \(1/e \approx 0.37\) at \(d = L\) and to \(e^{-4} \approx 0.02\) at \(d = 2L\), so \(L\) is the reach of a link. The Gaussian is the profile of a molecule that diffuses for a fixed time from one node towards the other, and \(L\) is the distance it typically travels in that time: as is standard for diffusion processes, \(L^2 = 4Dt\) for a diffusion coefficient \(D\) and a time \(t\).

    k-nearest-neighbour model¶

    Each BCN is linked to its edges.k nearest TGT. BCN degrees are exactly k; TGT degrees follow from the geometry, and the degrees section is not used.

    Background edges¶

    Background edges link pairs regardless of distance, and are stored in graph.background. Their number is background.ratio times the number of geometric edges, rounded down; graph.info["background_ratio"] records the ratio reached. background.model sets how the endpoints are drawn:

    • none (the default): no background edges;
    • uniform: every pair equally likely;

    • configuration: pair \((i, j)\) drawn with probability proportional to the geometric degrees of its nodes,

      \[ \Pr(i, j) \propto g_i\, g_j, \qquad g_i = \text{number of geometric edges of } i, \]

      so that every node gets on average the same share of background edges, ratio / (1 + ratio). Nodes without geometric edges get none.

    Pairs that are already linked are redrawn, so the two kinds of edges are disjoint; the origin column of graph.edges() tells them apart.

    Reproducibility¶

    One seed spawns independent random streams for positions, degrees, geometric edges and background edges (numpy.random.SeedSequence). The seed is seed in the specification (default 42), generate(spec, seed=...) overrides it, and graph.seed records the one used. Sinkhorn scaling runs on one BLAS thread, so its result does not depend on the number of cores. On a given machine and library build the same seed gives the same graph.

    Known limitations¶

    • Clipping lowers degrees. Sinkhorn scaling matches the targets before the clipping at 1. For kernels short compared with the distance between linked nodes, clipping removes edges: with 800 x 1000 nodes, lognormal degrees with BCN median 15 and sigma 0.5 (BCN) and 0.6 (TGT), and uniform positions, the expected number of edges is 90% of the target at scale 0.15 and 99% at scale 0.30. Broader degree distributions lose more. graph.info reports target_edges, the target number of edges, and expected_edges, the expected number after clipping.
    • Short kernels. When too few partners lie within the kernel range, the weights keep growing and Sinkhorn scaling stops at edges.sinkhorn.max_iter with a warning, also kept in graph.info["warning"]; if they overflow, generation stops with an error. Increase edges.scale or lower the degrees.
    • Memory. The kernel matrix is dense during scaling (\(4\,n_\mathrm{bcn} n_\mathrm{tgt}\) bytes).
    • Densities are measured on 2D points only: spec_from_graph accepts planar positions, while generation works in 2D and 3D. Measurement in 3D is planned for a future version.
    • Densities measured on points carry the assumptions listed in Observed graphs.
